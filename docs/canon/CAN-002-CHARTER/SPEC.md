# Atlas Charter

> This document is **Normative**.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Atlas Charter defines how Atlas Standards are governed.

Its purpose is to ensure transparency, consistency, and long-term sustainability.

---

# 2. Atlas Standards Organization

Atlas Standards consists of:

- Standards Council
- Working Groups
- Editors
- Reviewers
- Contributors

Each role has defined responsibilities.

---

# 3. Standards Council

The Standards Council is responsible for:

- Approving normative specifications
- Resolving architectural conflicts
- Maintaining the Constitution
- Managing official releases

The Council is the final authority on normative decisions.

---

# 4. Working Groups

Working Groups (WG) are responsible for developing standards in specific domains.

Initial Working Groups include:

- WG-Canon
- WG-Core
- WG-Information
- WG-Platform
- WG-Architecture
- WG-Security
- WG-Conformance
- WG-Documentation

Additional groups MAY be created.

---

# 5. Editors

Editors maintain specification documents.

Editors MAY:

- update drafts
- clarify wording
- improve examples

Editors SHALL NOT introduce normative changes without review.

---

# 6. Review Process

Every normative change SHALL follow:

Proposal

↓

Technical Review

↓

Public Review

↓

Approval

↓

Publication

No document becomes normative without approval.

---

# 7. Specification Lifecycle

Every specification progresses through:

Draft

↓

Review

↓

Candidate

↓

Stable

↓

Deprecated

↓

Archived

---

# 8. Decision Making

Decisions SHOULD be based on:

- technical merit
- interoperability
- long-term maintainability
- backward compatibility

---

# 9. Publication

Official publications SHALL:

- include version metadata
- include revision history
- include references
- identify dependencies

---

# 10. Amendment Process

This Charter MAY be amended only through the Atlas Governance Process.

Every amendment SHALL include:

- rationale
- impact assessment
- compatibility analysis
